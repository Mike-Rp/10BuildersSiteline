136	Body Text	This study developed 10BuildLine, a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. It aimed to address challenges in organizing project updates, personnel assignments, and documentation caused by manual communication. The system provided a centralized platform for managing construction projects and workforce coordination.
137	Body Text	The system was developed using the Agile methodology and technologies including React, TypeScript, JavaScript, Node.js, Express, and Supabase PostgreSQL. It supported four user roles: Administrator, Office Project Manager (OPM), Site Project Manager (SPM), and Client. Its features included project inquiries, automated workforce allocation, progress monitoring, document management, notifications, and report generation. Workforce allocation considered personnel qualifications, project complexity, and phases. SPMs submitted progress updates for OPM approval before client access.
138	Body Text	The developed system centralized project information, supported personnel coordination, and improved the organization of project records. It also facilitated structured progress reporting and controlled access to construction project information.

146	Heading 2	Project Context
147	Normal	The construction industry required companies to manage multiple ongoing projects while maintaining accurate personnel records, updated progress information, and organized documentation. Managing these responsibilities through manual processes, such as spreadsheets, verbal communication, and physical filing systems, presented challenges in monitoring construction activities and coordinating workforce assignments. The absence of a centralized system made it difficult to consolidate project information, track personnel availability, and maintain consistent communication among project stakeholders.
148	Normal	10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation experienced these operational challenges while handling approximately 30 to 50 construction projects annually, with several projects managed simultaneously. The company worked with over 50 Site Project Managers (SPMs), around 20 Office Project Managers (OPMs), and approximately 25 subcontractor companies. Despite managing numerous projects and personnel across different construction sites, the company relied on manual communication and recordkeeping for project monitoring and workforce coordination. Project updates were gathered and consolidated by Project Managers, making it difficult for the administration to obtain timely and organized information regarding ongoing construction activities.
149	Normal	Based on the interview with the company's representative, the organizational structure consisted of the Administrator, OPMs, and SPMs, with both project management roles performed by licensed engineers. The company handled client inquiries, project proposals, and personnel assignments through manual coordination. The absence of a structured workforce allocation process made it challenging to evaluate engineers based on their specialization, professional experience, PRC license validity, and availability. Furthermore, assigning an appropriate number of SPMs according to project complexity, scope, and construction phases required careful assessment to ensure that personnel assignments aligned with project requirements.
150	Normal	The company also relied on subcontractor companies to provide and manage construction laborers. Coordination with these companies was handled by SPMs, who communicated directly with designated subcontractor foremen regarding construction activities and workforce requirements. Subcontractor companies were responsible for managing their respective laborers, while the company focused on supervising project operations and coordinating engineering personnel. This organizational structure established the need for a system that supported the management of professional staff, subcontractor company records, and project assignments without including individual laborer scheduling and payroll management.
151	Normal	Additionally, project progress monitoring depended on communication between SPMs and OPMs. Field updates, photographs, and supporting documents were communicated and consolidated manually, making it challenging to maintain organized project records and provide clients with updated information. The lack of a centralized reporting and review process also created difficulties in tracking submitted updates, verifying construction progress, and maintaining consistent documentation throughout the project lifecycle.
152	Body Text	To address these challenges, this study developed 10BuildLine, a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. The system aimed to centralize project inquiries, personnel records, workforce assignments, construction progress updates, subcontractor information, and project documentation. It supported structured coordination among Administrators, Office Project Managers, Site Project Managers, and Clients through a unified platform. The developed system was intended to improve the organization and accessibility of project information while supporting workforce allocation, progress monitoring, and documentation management. 
153	Normal	Purpose and Description
154	Normal	This study aimed to develop 10BuildLine, a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. The system was designed to address the company's manual processes in handling project inquiries, assigning engineering personnel, monitoring construction progress, and managing project documentation. It provided a centralized platform that supported structured project coordination, automated workforce allocation, and progress monitoring through four user roles: Administrator, Office Project Manager (OPM), Site Project Manager (SPM), and Client.
155	Normal	The project inquiry process began with the Administrator recording the client's information, including the client's name, contact number, and email address, along with project details such as the project name, project type, land area (optional), lot area, floor area, number of floors, and location. Based on the submitted information and available personnel records, the system recommended a suitable OPM according to qualifications, relevant experience, and availability. The recommended OPM received a notification and could either accept or reject the inquiry. If rejected, the system automatically forwarded the inquiry to the next qualified OPM. Upon acceptance, the assigned OPM communicated directly with the client to discuss project requirements and prepare the necessary proposal.
156	Normal	Following the acceptance of the project proposal, the assigned OPM proceeded with project assessment and registration. The assessment considered the project's type, size, complexity, scope, and construction phases to determine its requirements. The approved project budget was also recorded and made accessible to the assigned OPM for reference, without requiring the system to calculate or distribute the budget across construction activities. These details served as the basis for project planning, estimated duration, and the subsequent allocation of engineering personnel.
157	Normal	Workforce deployment was facilitated through an automated allocation module that recommended qualified Site Project Managers (SPMs) based on their engineering discipline, specialization, professional experience, license validity, availability, and existing project assignments. The system covered six major engineering disciplines: Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, Electronics, Sanitary, and Geodetic Engineering, with specializations including structural, geotechnical, foundation, construction, HVAC, power systems, telecommunications, fire detection, plumbing, water supply, and land surveying. Rather than applying a fixed personnel allocation, the system determined the required number of SPMs based on project complexity and the engineering expertise needed for each construction phase. Qualified personnel could handle multiple responsibilities within the same project when appropriate, provided their qualifications and existing commitments allowed it. The OPM could review the system-generated recommendations, manually adjust assignments when necessary, and confirm the selected personnel. Designated SPMs were required to accept their assignments before deployment was finalized.
158	Normal	The system also supported subcontractor coordination through a designated Lead SPM. The Lead SPM was provided access to a centralized subcontractor directory containing registered subcontractor companies and their relevant information. This allowed the Lead SPM to identify and coordinate with suitable subcontractors based on project requirements. Subcontractor registration and credential management remained under the Administrator, while the subcontractor companies retained responsibility for managing their respective construction laborers. Individual laborer scheduling, payroll processing, and direct labor management were excluded from the system's scope.
159	Normal	Project monitoring was conducted through predefined construction phases and activity checklists. SPMs submitted progress updates, photographs, notes, and supporting documents as evidence of completed construction activities. These submissions were reviewed by the assigned OPM, who could approve them or return them for revision. Only approved activities contributed to the official project progress records. The system calculated completion percentages based on approved activities relative to the total predefined activities and compared actual progress with the planned project schedule. Succeeding construction phases remained restricted until the required activities of the preceding phase were completed. Approved progress information and authorized project documents were subsequently made accessible to clients through their designated dashboards.
160	Normal	All system functions operated under a role-based access structure. The Administrator managed client inquiries, user accounts, engineering personnel records, subcontractor registrations, and system-wide information. The OPM handled accepted inquiries, project proposals, assessments, SPM allocation, project schedules, and progress approvals. The SPM managed assigned field responsibilities, submitted construction updates, and coordinated project activities, while the designated Lead SPM additionally handled subcontractor coordination. The Client was provided access to approved project updates and authorized documents, ensuring that project information remained controlled and organized.
161	Normal	Through these integrated functions, 10BuildLine provided 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation with a centralized platform for managing project inquiries, coordinating engineering personnel, monitoring construction activities, and maintaining project documentation. The system aimed to support more organized project operations, structured workforce deployment, and consistent communication among authorized project stakeholders.

174	Normal	 Scope and Limitations
175	Normal	Scope
176	Normal	This capstone project developed a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system designed to address the absence of a centralized digital workflow within a single construction firm handles approximately 30 to 50 projects annually. The system consolidates five core operational functions — workforce allocation, subcontractor company management, project assignment tracking, construction document storage, and project timeline monitoring — into a unified role-governed platform.
177	List Paragraph	Engineering Personnel Database: The system maintains a centralized repository of records exclusive to professional engineering staff and Site Project Managers. Each record captures the individual's area of specialization such as Civil, Structural, Geotechnical, Mechanical, Electrical, and Sanitary, PRC license details, experience level, employment type, and current availability status. Record creation, modification, and deactivation are restricted to the Administrator role. 
178	List Paragraph	Subcontractor Company Database: The system maintains a directory of subcontractor companies, including their trade capabilities, contact details, foreman information, verification status, and project commitments. The Administrator registers companies and verifies their records before they become eligible for allocation through the directory 
179	List Paragraph	Automated Allocation Module: The system guides personnel deployment through a structured multi-stage process embedded in the project creation workflow. When a project is created, the system evaluates the available pool and presents the Administrator with a recommended Office Project Manager for confirmation. Once the Administrator confirms, the assigned OPM receives the project and is presented with a system-recommended Site Project Manager lineup for review, which the OPM may adjust within system-defined boundaries before confirming. Once the OPM confirms, the assigned SPM registers into the project scope and receives system-recommended subcontractor company deployments for review, which the SPM may likewise adjust within system-defined boundaries before finalizing. Human confirmation is required at every stage before the process advances, ensuring oversight is preserved throughout. 
180	List Paragraph	Field-to-Office Progress Reporting: Site Project Managers submit phase-level construction progress updates through a dedicated reporting module. Each submission enters a review queue accessible exclusively to the Office Project Manager, who reviews and either approves or returns the update before it is reflected in the official project timeline or made visible to the Client. This workflow ensures that only verified field data reaches official project records and external stakeholders. 
181	List Paragraph	Document Centralization: The system provides a centralized repository for project documents, including registered plans, permits, and agreements. Authorized staff can upload and retrieve documents according to their role and project permissions. Clients have view-only access to documents eligible for sharing on projects linked to their accounts. Internal contracts and supporting records are restricted according to their access rules. 
182	List Paragraph	In-App Notification Dashboard: The system provides in-app notifications for events such as assignment requests, approvals, project updates, and site issues. Notifications are loaded when users signed in. All alerts are contained within the platform and do not extend to external communication channels. 
183	List Paragraph	System Activity Logs: The system generates a permanent, timestamped record of significant events, personnel and subcon assignments, subcon record submissions and approvals, and document uploads. These logs are immutable and cannot be modified or deleted by any user role, including the Administrator. 
184	List Paragraph	On-Demand Report Generation: The system supports the generation of downloadable PDF summaries covering engineering workforce deployment, subcontractor allocation, and project progress. Reports reflect the state of data at the time of generation and are produced on demand rather than on a scheduled basis. 
185	List Paragraph	Automated Database Backup: The system incorporates an automated backup mechanism that executes scheduled captures of all operational data, including personnel records, subcontractor records, project assignments, audit logs, and uploaded documents. Administrators may initiate data restoration directly through the system dashboard. This mechanism operates within the bounds of the local hosting environment; offsite or cloud-redundant replication is outside the current scope.
186	List Paragraph	Data Security: All data transmission operates over HTTPS. Passwords are stored using industry-standard encryption protocols. Sessions expire automatically after a defined period of inactivity.
187	Normal	 Limitations
188	Normal	The developed system was limited to project monitoring and workforce allocation and did not include financial, accounting, procurement, payroll, or material inventory management. PRC license verification relied on manual review by the Administrator, while project progress tracking depended on updates submitted by Site Project Managers and verified by Office Project Managers rather than automated monitoring devices. Furthermore, notifications were limited to the in-app notification feature without SMS or email integration. As a web-based platform, the system required a stable internet connection and did not support offline functionality or a standalone mobile application.
189	List Paragraph	Exclusion of Financial, Accounting, and Logistics Modules: The system does not cover material inventory, procurement, payroll, subcontractor costs, or project budgeting. Contracts and agreements may be stored for documentation purposes, but financial calculations and transactions are outside the scope of the system.
190	List Paragraph	Manual License Verification: The verification of Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) licenses relies on the Administrator's manual review of submitted credentials. The system does not directly connect to the PRC database for automated license verification.
191	List Paragraph	Dependency on Manual Data Entry: The accuracy of project progress information depends on the updates submitted by Site Project Managers and reviewed by Office Project Managers. The system does not support automated progress tracking through IoT sensors or other external monitoring devices.
192	List Paragraph	In-App Notifications Only: The system provides notifications through its in-app notification feature. Users must access the platform to view their notifications, as SMS and email notifications are not included.
193	List Paragraph	Web-Based Platform and Internet Dependency: The system operates through web browsers and requires a stable internet connection. Although accessible through desktop and mobile browsers, it does not include a standalone mobile application or offline functionality.

197	Normal	Kim et al. (2024) argue that recurring quality and safety issues in construction projects are strongly linked to the adequacy of construction engineers’ experience, the number of field engineers, and the timing of their deployment at construction sites. Their findings emphasize increasing the number of engineers, particularly those at the intermediate level, and regulating site staffing more systematically. This perspective highlights human resource capacity as a primary determinant of project performance. However, while strengthening workforce structure may address operational gaps, it can be argued that quality problems do not solely stem from staffing shortages but may also be influenced by organizational culture, regulatory enforcement, and technological integration. Thus, improving engineer deployment alone may not fully resolve systemic safety and quality concerns.

199	Normal	Multi-objective Workforce Allocation in Construction Projects
200	Normal	In contrast, Iskandar et al. (2021) approach construction project management from a quantitative and optimization-based perspective. By modeling project management as a constrained multi objective optimization problem and applying a non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm, they demonstrate measurable improvements in cost, quality, and project duration. Their method reflects a data driven and algorithmic approach, suggesting that efficiency can be enhanced through advanced computational techniques. Nevertheless, while optimization models provide structured decision-making support, they may not fully capture unpredictable human factors, on-site complexities, and socio-organizational dynamics that influence project outcomes. Therefore, although both studies aim to improve construction performance, they differ in emphasis, with one prioritizing workforce adequacy and institutional regulation, and the other focusing on mathematical optimization and technological intervention. Together, these perspectives illustrate that effective construction management likely requires an integration of human resource strategies and analytical decision-making tools rather than reliance on a single approach.

214	Normal	Conceptual Framework of a Simulation-Based Manpower Planning Method for Construction Enterprises
215	Normal	Biruk (2022) proposed a method to support workforce demand planning specifically for construction contractors, addressing the common issue of skilled labor shortages in the industry. The method allows for analyzing project-to-project resource sharing and the uncertainties associated with irregular new orders, which are typical in construction. Since manpower planning is usually based on production plans, precise planning is feasible only for a short horizon with fixed orders, while new orders vary in timing, type, and volume. To manage this uncertainty, the study advocates using a simulation model of the company’s production plan, which maps variability in order numbers, start dates, delivery times, and workloads. Simulating prospective orders enables assessment of workforce utilization and the impact of staffing levels on project timeliness, providing a basis for determining appropriate employment levels. The approach also supports staff development, recruitment planning, and decisions regarding the use of in-house resources versus subcontractors. Overall, the method offers a tool for construction managers to make informed human resource decisions, accounting for risks and variability in project execution.

237	Normal	Synthesis
238	Normal	The reviewed literature, both foreign and local, converges on the premise that effective human resource allocation is a critical determinant of successful project execution, particularly within the construction and engineering sectors. Kim et al. (2024) and Amin et al. (2024) establish that structured, credential-verified deployment of personnel directly influences site outcomes, while Putri et al. (2024) and Dabirian et al. (2022) reinforce those findings by demonstrating that labor allocation, when properly managed, significantly affects both cost efficiency and timely project completion. Collectively, these studies affirm that workforce planning is not a peripheral concern but a foundational requirement for operational success. 
239	Normal	At the same time, several studies illustrate a shift from static, manual staffing practices toward dynamic, data-driven approaches. Iskandar et al. (2021) and Biruk (2022) show that optimization models and simulation techniques can substantially improve decision-making in personnel assignment, while Wang et al. (2021) extends this discussion by addressing workforce allocation under crisis conditions, such as the COVID-19 pandemic. These contributions suggest that modern project management increasingly depends on systems capable of adapting to fluctuating demands and unforeseen disruptions, rather than relying solely on conventional, experience-based staffing decisions.
240	Normal	Local studies further contextualize these findings within the Philippine setting. Carillo et al. (2025) and Cantara et al. (2022) reveal that government and engineering offices continue to experience staffing insufficiencies and inefficient resource distribution, often due to the absence of structured workforce planning tools. Hernandez (2025) adds that while digital monitoring platforms such as the City Engineering Project Monitoring System have improved certain aspects of project oversight, they remain limited in scope, particularly in integrating personnel credentialing with real-time allocation and reporting functions. This indicates that although digitalization efforts are underway locally, they have yet to fully address the interconnected challenges of workforce management and project monitoring. 
241	Normal	Taken together, the literature reveals a consistent gap: no existing system, whether foreign or local, offers a unified platform that integrates credentialed personnel records, skill-based multi-stage allocation, field-to-office reporting, and centralized document management within a single governed structure. Existing solutions tend to address these components in isolation, either focusing on optimization algorithms, workforce planning models, or monitoring systems, but rarely as a cohesive whole. It is this gap that the developed system directly addresses, drawing on the strengths identified across the reviewed studies, structured deployment, dynamic allocation logic, and digital monitoring, while consolidating them into a single, integrated platform designed to improve efficiency, accountability, and responsiveness in construction project workforce management.

705	Normal	Description of the Prototype
706	Normal	10BuildLine was developed as a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. It provided four role-based workspaces for Administrators, Office Project Managers, Site Project Managers, and Clients. Administrators recorded and routed project inquiries, managed accounts, monitored active sites, and viewed submitted phase evidence and its review history. The assigned Office Project Manager handled the client proposal, confirmed project details and budget, assessed project requirements, and sent assignment offers to Site Project Managers. When an inquiry was finalized with a valid, unused email address, the system set up the client account. Site Project Managers responded to assignment offers, viewed their assigned phases, and submitted checklist evidence. The designated Lead Site Project Manager coordinated subcontractor allocation for the project. The assigned Office Project Manager reviewed submitted evidence and either approved it or returned it with a reason for correction. Clients accessed a separate portal to view project progress, site photographs, and documents shared with their account.
707	Normal	The prototype required an internet-connected computer or mobile device with a web browser. Site Project Managers could use a camera or upload existing image files as photographic evidence. The front end was developed using React, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS, with Vite as the build tool. The back end used Node.js and Express. PostgreSQL stored project and account records, while Supabase Storage was configured for uploaded files. The application was configured for deployment on Vercel.
708	Normal	Automated functional tests covered role-based sign-in, inquiry routing and finalization, assignment offers, client account setup, subcontractor allocation, evidence review, and client access. These tests checked whether approved checklist evidence contributed to verified progress, whether returned evidence retained the reason for correction, and whether clients could access records shared with their account.
709	Normal	The prototype was designed to support pilot evaluation through tasks performed within each role-based workspace and the collection of user feedback on its functionality, ease of use, and suitability for the company’s project monitoring and workforce allocation process. 
710	Normal	Implementation Plan
711	Normal	The implementation plan for 10BuildLine outlined the deployment of the web application and its back-end services on Vercel, with PostgreSQL for application records and Supabase Storage for uploaded files. Initial verification was planned to cover user sign-in, access to the appropriate workspace, and file uploads and retrieval within role permissions. The system was designed for access through web browsers on office computers or mobile devices.
712	Normal	The rollout was planned to begin with one construction project. The Administrator would prepare staff accounts and subcontractor records, record client and project details as an inquiry, and route it to the recommended Office Project Manager. The assigned Office Project Manager would record the client’s proposal decision, review project details and budget, complete the assessment, confirm the signed client contract, and send assignment offers to qualified Site Project Managers. Finalizing the inquiry with a valid, unused email address would create and link the client account. The Office Project Manager would then issue an activation code for the client to set up their sign-in.
713	Normal	The orientation plan covered the respective tasks of the Administrator, Office Project Manager, Site Project Managers, and participating client. The Lead Site Project Manager would coordinate subcontractor allocation, while Site Project Managers would accept their assignments and submit photographs and other evidence for assigned checklist activities. The assigned Office Project Manager would approve submissions or return them with a reason for correction. Approved checklist evidence would contribute to verified progress, and the Administrator would have access to the evidence and decision history for oversight. Once the project was active and client access was established, the client would view progress information, site photographs, and documents shared with their account.
714	Normal	The identified implementation requirements included office computers, camera-equipped mobile devices for field staff, reliable internet access, and access to hosting, database, and file storage services. The company office and selected construction site were designated as the locations for orientation and pilot use. Planned training materials included user guides, the pilot project schedule, phase checklists, and sample documents.

1125	Normal	Conclusion
1126	Normal	The evaluation of 10BuildLine indicated that the system supported the project monitoring and workforce allocation tasks assessed at 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. The survey included 42 respondents across four user roles: one Administrator, 11 Office Project Managers, 23 Site Project Managers, and seven Clients. All recorded responses across the five objectives and the overall feedback section were either Agree or Strongly Agree. All respondents strongly agreed that the system was easy to use and that they were satisfied with their overall experience.
1127	Normal	The findings for centralized records and workforce allocation indicated that respondents could locate relevant records and identify personnel responsibilities. The Administrator strongly agreed that engineer and subcontractor records were easy to find and that the system displayed engineers recorded work experience. Office Project Managers strongly agreed that the system helped them identify eligible Site Project Managers, engineering scopes requiring coverage, the designated Lead Site Project Manager, and assignment acceptance. Site Project Managers strongly agreed that their assigned projects, phases, and responsibilities were clearly presented and that assignment offers were easy to respond to.
1128	Normal	The results for project progress reporting and review supported the system’s evidence submission and approval workflow. Site Project Managers strongly agreed that they could identify checklist activities open for submission, submit supporting evidence, and find the Office Project Manager’s reason when evidence was returned for correction. Office Project Managers strongly agreed that evidence could be returned with a reason and that the system showed how approval affected verified progress. Ten of the 11 Office Project Managers (90.91%) strongly agreed that evidence awaiting review was easy to find, while one (9.09%) agreed. All seven Clients strongly agreed that the portal clearly displayed the current project phase, presented overall progress understandably, and made shared updates easy to find.
1129	Normal	The findings for role-based access and system support features indicated that respondents understood their access and could use the functions available in their respective workspaces. However, some record-retrieval items received less favorable ratings than others. The Administrator selected Agree for finding project documents and reviewing previous progress updates. Three Office Project Managers (27.27%) selected Agree for finding the signed client contract, while eight (72.73%) selected Strongly Agree. These findings suggested opportunities to improve document navigation and the visibility of records awaiting review.
1130	Normal	Overall, the survey findings supported the suitability of 10BuildLine for the workflows evaluated by the participating users. The system provided a shared platform for maintaining project records, coordinating personnel and subcontractor assignments, submitting and reviewing checklist evidence, and sharing authorized project information with Clients. These conclusions reflected users’ reported experiences during the evaluation; further use across additional projects would help assess sustained operational benefits and identify additional improvements.
1131	Normal	Recommendations
1132	Normal	Based on the evaluation findings, future researchers may investigate the following enhancements to extend 10BuildLine’s project monitoring and workforce allocation capabilities.
1133	Normal	Offline Field Reporting and Synchronization. Future researchers may develop offline support that allows Site Project Managers to prepare reports, capture photographs, and save evidence drafts during internet interruptions. Saved submissions could synchronize when connectivity is restored, with safeguards against duplicate uploads and conflicting changes.
1134	Normal	Construction Cost and Budget Monitoring. Future researchers may extend the existing project budget information to include actual expenses, approved variations, and budget comparisons. This could help project teams examine financial performance alongside verified construction progress.
1135	Normal	Materials and Inventory Management. Future researchers may develop a module for tracking material requests, deliveries, stock levels, and consumption. These records could help project teams identify shortages, excess purchases, and differences between delivered and consumed quantities.
1136	Normal	Construction Safety Management. Future researchers may introduce a dedicated module for reporting hazards, recording incidents and near misses, and monitoring corrective actions. This could strengthen safety oversight alongside the system’s progress and evidence review workflows.
1137	Normal	Construction Dependency and Delay Analysis. Future researchers may investigate tools for defining dependencies between construction activities and examining how delays could affect succeeding work. This could help project managers identify critical activities, assess schedule risks, and plan recovery actions.
1138	Normal	
1139	Normal	Post-Construction Maintenance and Warranty Management. Future researchers may extend the system to support activities after project turnover, including warranty tracking, defect reporting, maintenance scheduling, and service requests. This could preserve relevant project records for building owners and maintenance personnel.
1140	Normal	Environmental and Construction Waste Monitoring. Future researchers may develop tools for recording construction waste, disposal methods, water consumption, and energy use. These records could support the assessment of resource use and environmental practices across construction projects.
1141	Normal	Document Scanning and Text Recognition. Future researchers may investigate camera-based document scanning with page-edge detection, perspective correction, image enhancement, and multipage PDF creation. Optical character recognition could support searchable document text and suggest details for user verification before saving.
1142	Normal	Subcontractor Laborer Attendance and Deployment. Future researchers may extend subcontractor records to support individual laborer attendance, daily deployment, and actual manpower reporting by trade and site. This could help project teams compare the workforce supplied with planned project requirements.
1143	Normal	Location-Based Project Monitoring. Future researchers may introduce an interactive map showing project locations, status, verified progress, and assigned teams. Geotagged photographs and field updates could provide additional context for authorized personnel monitoring dispersed sites and planning site visits.
