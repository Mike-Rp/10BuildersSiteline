ABSTRACT

Title of research:A Web-Based Project Monitoring and Workforce Allocation for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation

Researchers:Bontigao, Ria Ann Kate
Cruzada, France Czyrus
Miculob, Mark Angelo
Villar, Beatrice Marie

Degree:Bachelor of Science in Information Technology
Date of Completion:November 2026
Keywords:Construction Project Monitoring, Progress Updates, Web-Based Workforce Allocation, Centralized System

This study developed 10BuildLine, a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. The system was designed to address the company's reliance on manual communication for project updates, which made information difficult to organize, track, and access. It provided a centralized platform for managing project progress, personnel assignments, and related documents.
10BuildLine supported four user roles: Administrator, Office Project Manager, Site Project Manager, and Client. Site Project Managers submitted progress updates, photographs, notes, and checklist evidence, while Office Project Managers reviewed and approved submissions before they became visible to clients. The system also included workforce allocation, project dashboards, document management, notifications, and progress reports. It was developed using the Agile methodology with React, TypeScript, JavaScript, Node.js, Express, and Supabase PostgreSQL.
The developed platform centralized project records and supported controlled reporting, personnel coordination, and access to approved updates.

INTRODUCTION
Project Context

The construction industry demands that firms simultaneously manage multiple active projects while keeping personnel records accurate, progress data current, and documentation practices consistent. When these responsibilities are handled through manual processes such as spreadsheets, verbal reporting, and physical filing systems, the outcome is fragmented visibility, unreliable records, and operational decisions made without sufficient information.
10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation operated under these conditions. The company handled approximately 30 to 50 construction projects annually, with multiple projects being managed simultaneously. These projects were supported by a workforce consisting of over 50 Site Project Managers, around 20 Office Project Managers, and approximately 25 subcontractor companies. Despite managing this volume of projects and coordinating multiple personnel across different construction sites, the firm operated without a centralized digital system. Project progress monitoring depended entirely on manual consolidation by Project Managers, leaving administrators without a timely or unified view of project health across ongoing projects. Engineering personnel deployment remained unstructured, with no formal mechanism for evaluating an engineer's specialization, PRC license standing, experience level, and availability before assignment. Furthermore, the firm lacked a defined channel through which Site Project Managers could submit verified field progress to Office Project Managers for review before the information was reflected in official project records or shared with clients.
The interview with the firm's representative confirmed that the in-house workforce consisted exclusively of the Administrator, Office Project Managers (OPMs), and Site Project Managers (SPMs), with both OPMs and SPMs being licensed engineers. All construction laborers operated under subcontractor companies, which were responsible for managing their respective workers. Communication with these subcontractor companies was handled exclusively by the Site Project Managers, who directly coordinated with the designated foremen of each subcontractor company regarding project-related activities and workforce requirements. Furthermore, subcontractor companies were registered in the system exclusively by the Administrator after reviewing and verifying their credentials to ensure their eligibility for project allocation. This organizational structure defined the scope of the proposed system, focusing on the management and allocation of in-house professional staff and registered subcontractor companies, while excluding labor scheduling, individual laborer management, and payroll processes, which remained under the responsibility of the subcontractor companies.
This study therefore proposed the development of a Web-Based Project Monitoring and Workforce Allocation System for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. The system centralized engineering personnel records, project progress data, field submissions, subcontractor company records, assignment histories, and construction documents within a single governed platform, supported by a four-role access structure covering Administrators, Office Project Managers, Site Project Managers, and Clients.
Purpose and Description
This study aimed to develop a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation, replacing the firm's existing manual workflows with a structured digital platform organized around four operational functions and four user roles.
Field progress reporting was formalized through a dedicated module where Site Project Managers submitted construction progress updates based on predefined activity checklists within each project phase. Each construction phase consisted of a fixed set of predefined activities that served as the basis for monitoring project progress. Site Project Managers reported the completion of these activities and submitted supporting evidence for verification by the assigned Office Project Manager, who could approve or return the submissions for revision. Progress within each phase was displayed as the number of approved completed activities relative to the total number of predefined activities. The system also implemented sequential phase progression, where succeeding construction phases remained locked until the preceding phase had been completed. Furthermore, overall project completion was calculated by dividing the total number of approved completed activities by the total number of predefined activities across all construction phases and multiplying the result by 100. This actual completion rate was compared with the planned completion based on the project schedule, allowing authorized personnel to determine whether the project was ahead of, behind, or aligned with its expected timeline. This structured approach ensured that only verified activity completion contributed to official project progress records and information shared with clients.
Personnel deployment was facilitated through an automated workforce allocation module integrated into the project intake process. The workflow began with the Administrator entering essential project information, including the project name, project type (residential, commercial, or mixed-use), start date, estimated completion date, floor area in square meters, number of floors, and client name. To accommodate recurring clients, the system allowed the Administrator to select an existing client account or create a new one, maintaining organized client and project records. Upon completing the project intake, the system automatically generated Office Project Manager (OPM) and Site Project Manager (SPM) allocation recommendations. For OPM allocation, the system prioritized licensed engineers based on their years of experience in the corresponding project type, ranking those with the most relevant experience first. The highest-ranked eligible OPM was recommended for assignment, while the Administrator retained the authority to rerun the allocation process or manually select another OPM when necessary.
For SPM allocation, the system generated a phase-based engineering team according to the specializations required by each construction phase, including Civil, Structural, Geotechnical, Electrical, Mechanical, and Plumbing. The allocation process considered specialization coverage and personnel availability, allowing a qualified SPM to handle multiple responsibilities and participate in succeeding phases within the same project. However, an SPM who was already committed to a project could not be allocated to another project until their existing commitment had ended. For phases requiring five to six specializations, the system initially attempted to achieve full specialization coverage using two engineers, expanding to three when necessary. If full coverage could not be achieved, the system applied a minimum requirement of covering at least half of the required specializations. The system also estimated engineering workload based on the required discipline workload slots and the project's size multiplier, without treating these slots as the actual number of individual engineers needed. The generated assignments and corresponding responsibilities were presented for the Administrator's review and adjustment before confirmation.
At the final stage of project intake, the Administrator attached the required client contract agreement and indicated whether the client had their own subcontractor company. Once the project details, personnel allocations, and required documentation had been reviewed, the Administrator confirmed the project intake and forwarded it to the assigned Office Project Manager. The system implemented an assignment acceptance process in which both the designated Office Project Manager and Site Project Managers had to acknowledge and accept their respective project assignments before deployment was finalized. This ensured that personnel assignments were not automatically considered confirmed upon administrative selection and that the designated engineers had formally accepted their project responsibilities. Through these processes, the system supported structured project registration, specialization-based workforce allocation, personnel availability management, and coordinated project deployment while maintaining administrative oversight and personnel accountability.
All system functions were governed by a four-role access structure. The Administrator managed user accounts, oversaw engineering credential records, and monitored system-wide activity logs. The Office Project Manager held functional authority over project timelines, document approvals, engineer assignments, and field submission reviews across all active projects. The Site Project Manager was limited to submitting progress updates and field documents for their assigned projects only. The Client was granted view-only access to approved progress data and project documents. 
By consolidating these functions into a single governed platform, the system equipped 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation with the operational visibility, structured reporting mechanisms, and workforce coordination capabilities that its current manual processes were unable to provide.
Objectives of the Study

General Objective
The main objective of this study is to design and develop a Web-Based Project Monitoring and Workforce Allocation System for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation that enhances organizational efficiency through structured project oversight, informed personnel deployment, and governed role-based access control.
Specific Objectives
To design and develop a centralized web-based platform that replaces fragmented manual workflows with a structured digital system for managing engineering personnel and subcontractor company records, tracking project progress, and consolidating construction documents, formal agreements, and contracts within a single governed environment. 
To implement an automated workforce allocation module that guides personnel deployment through a structured multi-stage process in which the Administrator confirms a system-recommended Office Project Manager, the OPM confirms a system-recommended Site Project Manager lineup, and the SPM confirms system-recommended subcontractor deployments, with human confirmation required at every stage before the process advances.
To establish a field-to-office progress reporting workflow in which Site Project Managers submit phase-level updates for Office Project Manager review and approval before any data is published to the official project timeline or made visible to the Client, ensuring that only verified field data enters the official record.
To enforce a four-role access control structure governing the distinct permissions of the Administrator, Office Project Manager, Site Project Manager, and Client across all system functions, ensuring data integrity and operational accountability throughout all user interactions.
To integrate system support features covering in-app notifications, on-demand PDF report generation, an immutable audit log, and an automated database backup mechanism that collectively ensure operational transparency, data reliability, and recovery readiness across the entire platform.
Scope and Limitations
Scope
This capstone project developed a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system designed to address the absence of a centralized digital workflow within a single construction firm handles approximately 30 to 50 projects annually. The system consolidates five core operational functions — workforce allocation, subcontractor company management, project assignment tracking, construction document storage, and project timeline monitoring — into a unified role-governed platform.
Engineering Personnel Database: The system maintains a centralized repository of records exclusive to professional engineering staff and Site Project Managers. Each record captures the individual's area of specialization such as Civil, Structural, Geotechnical, Mechanical, Electrical, and Sanitary, PRC license details, experience level, employment type, and current availability status. Record creation, modification, and deactivation are restricted to the Administrator role. 
Subcontractor Company Database: The system maintains a directory of subcontractor companies, including their trade capabilities, contact details, foreman information, verification status, and project commitments. The Administrator registers companies and verifies their records before they become eligible for allocation through the directory 
Automated Allocation Module: The system guides personnel deployment through a structured multi-stage process embedded in the project creation workflow. When a project is created, the system evaluates the available pool and presents the Administrator with a recommended Office Project Manager for confirmation. Once the Administrator confirms, the assigned OPM receives the project and is presented with a system-recommended Site Project Manager lineup for review, which the OPM may adjust within system-defined boundaries before confirming. Once the OPM confirms, the assigned SPM registers into the project scope and receives system-recommended subcontractor company deployments for review, which the SPM may likewise adjust within system-defined boundaries before finalizing. Human confirmation is required at every stage before the process advances, ensuring oversight is preserved throughout. 
Field-to-Office Progress Reporting: Site Project Managers submit phase-level construction progress updates through a dedicated reporting module. Each submission enters a review queue accessible exclusively to the Office Project Manager, who reviews and either approves or returns the update before it is reflected in the official project timeline or made visible to the Client. This workflow ensures that only verified field data reaches official project records and external stakeholders. 
Document Centralization: The system provides a centralized repository for project documents, including registered plans, permits, and agreements. Authorized staff can upload and retrieve documents according to their role and project permissions. Clients have view-only access to documents eligible for sharing on projects linked to their accounts. Internal contracts and supporting records are restricted according to their access rules. 
In-App Notification Dashboard: The system provides in-app notifications for events such as assignment requests, approvals, project updates, and site issues. Notifications are loaded when users signed in. All alerts are contained within the platform and do not extend to external communication channels. 
System Activity Logs: The system generates a permanent, timestamped record of significant events, personnel and subcon assignments, subcon record submissions and approvals, and document uploads. These logs are immutable and cannot be modified or deleted by any user role, including the Administrator. 
On-Demand Report Generation: The system supports the generation of downloadable PDF summaries covering engineering workforce deployment, subcontractor allocation, and project progress. Reports reflect the state of data at the time of generation and are produced on demand rather than on a scheduled basis. 
Automated Database Backup and Recovery: The system incorporates an automated backup mechanism that executes scheduled captures of all operational data, including personnel records, subcontractor records, project assignments, audit logs, and uploaded documents. Administrators may initiate data restoration directly through the system dashboard. This mechanism operates within the bounds of the local hosting environment; offsite or cloud-redundant replication is outside the current scope. 
Data Security: All data transmission operates over HTTPS. Passwords are stored using industry-standard encryption protocols. Sessions expire automatically after a defined period of inactivity.
Limitations 
Exclusion of Financial, Accounting, and Logistics Modules: The system does not cover material inventory, procurement, payroll, subcontractor costs, or project budgeting. Contracts and agreements may be stored for documentation purposes, but financial calculations and transactions are outside the scope of the system.
Manual License Verification: The verification of Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) licenses relies on the Administrator's manual review of submitted credentials. The system does not directly connect to the PRC database for automated license verification.
Dependency on Manual Data Entry: The accuracy of project progress information depends on the updates submitted by Site Project Managers and reviewed by Office Project Managers. The system does not support automated progress tracking through IoT sensors or other external monitoring devices.
In-App Notifications Only: The system provides notifications through its in-app notification feature. Users must access the platform to view their notifications, as SMS and email notifications are not included.
Web-Based Platform and Internet Dependency: The system operates through web browsers and requires a stable internet connection. Although accessible through desktop and mobile browsers, it does not include a standalone mobile application or offline functionality.
Review of Related Literature/Studies/Systems
Foreign Review of Related Literature

Deployment of Appropriate Architectural Construction Engineers to Solve Quality and Safety Problems at Construction Sites
Kim et al. (2024) argue that recurring quality and safety issues in construction projects are strongly linked to the adequacy of construction engineers’ experience, the number of field engineers, and the timing of their deployment at construction sites. Their findings emphasize increasing the number of engineers, particularly those at the intermediate level, and regulating site staffing more systematically. This perspective highlights human resource capacity as a primary determinant of project performance. However, while strengthening workforce structure may address operational gaps, it can be argued that quality problems do not solely stem from staffing shortages but may also be influenced by organizational culture, regulatory enforcement, and technological integration. Thus, improving engineer deployment alone may not fully resolve systemic safety and quality concerns.

Multi-objective Workforce Allocation in Construction Projects
In contrast, Iskandar et al. (2021) approach construction project management from a quantitative and optimization-based perspective. By modeling project management as a constrained multi objective optimization problem and applying a non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm, they demonstrate measurable improvements in cost, quality, and project duration. Their method reflects a data driven and algorithmic approach, suggesting that efficiency can be enhanced through advanced computational techniques. Nevertheless, while optimization models provide structured decision-making support, they may not fully capture unpredictable human factors, on-site complexities, and socio-organizational dynamics that influence project outcomes. Therefore, although both studies aim to improve construction performance, they differ in emphasis, with one prioritizing workforce adequacy and institutional regulation, and the other focusing on mathematical optimization and technological intervention. Together, these perspectives illustrate that effective construction management likely requires an integration of human resource strategies and analytical decision-making tools rather than reliance on a single approach.

Optimizing Human Resource Allocation in Construction Projects in Indonesia: A Comprehensive Analysis of Influencing Factors
Putri et al. (2024) highlighted that the Indonesian construction sector, driven by population growth and infrastructure needs, recovered after the second-quarter 2020 slowdown caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, achieving project completions totaling Rp1,311.4 trillion. They emphasized that human resource allocation is critical in this labor-intensive industry, as it directly affects project efficiency and overall performance. The study also explored factors influencing HR selection and deployment, addressing challenges of workforce shortages or excess, and proposed a framework for assessing how HR, cost, and company-related factors impact project performance.

Optimizing Human Resource Allocation in Construction Projects: A Case Study
Amin et al. (2024) emphasized the critical role of human resources in ensuring the successful implementation of construction projects, highlighting that workers must possess the appropriate skills and competencies aligned with specific job descriptions from project initiation to completion. Their study assessed the feasibility of constructing the Iskandar Muda Military Regional Finance Office in Banda Aceh City from a human resource perspective. Based on productivity analysis, they identified a decline in the relationship between project duration and work productivity, indicating that the existing workforce was not fully effective in supporting construction feasibility. Although 32 workers were assigned skills corresponding to their roles, the findings underscored the need to optimize labor allocation and improve time utilization. The study concluded that applying more efficient work methods for repetitive tasks and adjusting labor requirements throughout the project timeline can significantly enhance productivity and overall project outcomes.

Sustainable allocation model of construction workforce for work resumption during COVID-19
Wang et al. (2021) examined the impact of COVID-19 on the construction industry, particularly the challenges related to strict pandemic control measures, labor sustainability, and minimizing project losses. To address these issues, they developed a decision model aimed at optimizing workforce allocation while balancing pandemic prevention with project resumption. The study evaluated project resumption priorities based on project characteristics, strategic considerations, and regional pandemic conditions. It also assessed skilled workers’ energy levels in relation to team size, expertise, and experience to support sustainable workforce deployment. Using a cellular automaton simulation technique, Wang et al. (2021) analyzed allocation pathways, energy similarity functions, and time cost relationships to design sustainable resumption plans. Their case study of SGJ Construction demonstrated that the proposed model effectively supports workforce allocation decisions during pandemic conditions while promoting balanced interests between construction firms and society. The findings contribute to the broader discussion on adaptive workforce management strategies in crisis situations.

Dynamic modelling of human resource allocation in construction projects
Dabirian et al. (2022) emphasized that the success of construction projects largely depends on effective human resource management, particularly the proper allocation of labor. They identified workforce allocation as a critical managerial process that significantly influences project performance in terms of cost efficiency and timely completion. Recognizing the complexity and dynamic nature of labor allocation, including feedback mechanisms and interrelated project variables, the study proposed a dynamic model based on a system dynamics approach to improve workforce planning. According to Dabirian et al. (2022), the proposed model enables more accurate estimation of labor requirements and supports effective allocation strategies throughout the project lifecycle. By facilitating advance planning and continuous monitoring of workforce supply, the model enhances decision making related to on-time labor provision and overall project execution. The findings contribute to the development of more structured and policy driven approaches to workforce planning in construction management.

Conceptual Framework of a Simulation-Based Manpower Planning Method for Construction Enterprises
Biruk (2022) proposed a method to support workforce demand planning specifically for construction contractors, addressing the common issue of skilled labor shortages in the industry. The method allows for analyzing project-to-project resource sharing and the uncertainties associated with irregular new orders, which are typical in construction. Since manpower planning is usually based on production plans, precise planning is feasible only for a short horizon with fixed orders, while new orders vary in timing, type, and volume. To manage this uncertainty, the study advocates using a simulation model of the company’s production plan, which maps variability in order numbers, start dates, delivery times, and workloads. Simulating prospective orders enables assessment of workforce utilization and the impact of staffing levels on project timeliness, providing a basis for determining appropriate employment levels. The approach also supports staff development, recruitment planning, and decisions regarding the use of in-house resources versus subcontractors. Overall, the method offers a tool for construction managers to make informed human resource decisions, accounting for risks and variability in project execution. 

Agile Project Management for Sustainable Construction: A Systematic and Thematic Literature Review
Zamil and Alhusban (2025), in their study entitled Agile Project Management for Sustainable Construction: A Systematic and Thematic Literature Review, examined the application of Agile project management practices in the construction industry. Through a review of 104 scholarly articles, the researchers highlighted the importance of flexible planning, effective communication, stakeholder collaboration, and digital tools in improving construction project management. Their findings emphasized that Agile practices can support better project coordination, decision-making, and adaptability throughout the construction process. This study is relevant to the development of 10BuildLine, a Web-Based Project Monitoring and Workforce Allocation System, as it highlights the importance of organized communication and coordination among project stakeholders. Similarly, 10BuildLine provides a centralized platform for monitoring project progress, managing workforce assignments, and reviewing submitted updates. Although the study focuses on Agile practices in sustainable construction, its findings support the need for improved collaboration and information sharing in construction project management.

However, the study also identified significant challenges, including uncontrolled scope expansion, inaccurate time estimations, and inefficient task distribution, which contributed to project delays. Short-term coping mechanisms, such as reprioritizing projects, extending deadlines, and requiring overtime work, were commonly applied but were deemed unsustainable. The authors recommended structured strategies to improve Agile implementation and ensure more effective project outcomes.

Local Review of Related Literature

Workforce Analysis of the Procurement Office at Visayas State University- Isabel
Carillo et al. (2025) investigated the staffing issues experienced by the Procurement Office of Visayas State University–Isabel Campus. The study focused on identifying the appropriate number of personnel needed to improve work efficiency and reduce operational difficulties. A 14-day work sampling method was conducted, which recorded 1,348 observations involving four staff members. The results revealed that the employees’ productivity ranged from 90.21% to 93.77%, which is significantly higher than the standard productivity range of 70–75%. This finding suggested that the employees were handling an excessive workload that could lead to fatigue and job-related stress. The workforce analysis produced a score of 5.052, indicating that the current staffing level was insufficient for the office’s demands. As a result, the study recommended adding one more staff member to improve workload distribution and support more efficient office operations.

Navigating Human Resource Dynamics in Construction Industry during the COVID-19 Pandemic
Signe et al. (2024) explored how human resource management responded to the sudden disruptions in the construction industry during the COVID-19 lockdown in Cebu, Philippines. The research focused on understanding the experiences of both human resource practitioners and construction workers during the pandemic. Using purposive sampling, the researchers conducted in-depth interviews with seven HR practitioners and twelve construction workers, and the collected data were analyzed using the Collaizzi method. The results revealed several difficulties faced by HR practitioners, such as work interruptions, limited financial resources for HR operations, the need to reorganize workforce distribution, and strict compliance with health protocols. On the other hand, construction workers experienced fear, uncertainty, loss of income, and the need to adapt creatively to challenging conditions. The findings highlighted the significance of effective human resource strategies in managing workforce challenges during times of crisis. Consequently, the study suggested developing a Human Resource Crisis Management Model to help organizations improve resilience and strengthen crisis management practices in the construction industry.
Program for the Task Allocation Model: Integrating Workforce Planning for Manpower Utilization at City of Cabuyao Engineering Office
Cantara et al. (2022) investigated the challenges faced by a City Engineering Office in handling construction projects, particularly the accumulation of pending work. A survey of seven employees revealed that limited labor capacity was a key factor contributing to delays in project allocation. The study emphasized that effective strategic planning requires having the right number of workers with appropriate skills in the right roles at the proper time. To address these issues, the researchers proposed an Integrated Workforce Planning Model to improve the distribution of personnel. The model utilized an Excel Visual Basic Application-based system to facilitate workforce allocation and project management. Data analysis included factors such as workforce capacity, utilization points, budget limits, and estimated project durations. The findings indicated that implementing workforce planning is an effective approach to optimize staff capacity and ensure the timely completion of construction projects.

Monitoring for results: An evaluation of City Engineering Project Monitoring System (CEPMS) in Quezon City
Hernandez (2025) evaluated the City Engineering Project Monitoring System (CEPMS) as a digital platform used by the Quezon City Department of Engineering. The study focused on assessing users’ perceptions of the system in terms of outcome achievement, operational efficiency, service delivery quality, and overall trust and legitimacy. It also examined the strengths and weaknesses observed throughout the project monitoring process. Furthermore, the research investigated the challenges encountered by users in the implementation and daily use of the system. The findings highlighted areas where CEPMS performed effectively, particularly in streamlining monitoring tasks and improving data accessibility. At the same time, certain limitations were identified that affected user experience and overall efficiency. Hernandez emphasized the need to address these challenges to enhance the effectiveness and reliability of digital monitoring platforms in engineering project management.

Segmentation study of Filipino government workers on telecommuting impacts during the COVID-19 pandemic
Fernandez-Abila (2025) explained that telecommuting among Filipino government employees during the COVID-19 pandemic resulted in different experiences that affected their work responsibilities, personal lives, and working environments. Through a nationwide survey and cluster analysis, the study identified three groups of employees—advocates, reluctant, and critics—based on their perceptions of remote work. The findings highlight that while many employees recognized the benefits of telecommuting and supported its continued implementation, others experienced challenges that influenced their attitudes toward remote work. The study also emphasized that understanding these differences can help government agencies create more effective policies related to workforce management, performance evaluation, and future work arrangements in the public sector.

Synthesis
The reviewed literature, both foreign and local, converges on the premise that effective human resource allocation is a critical determinant of successful project execution, particularly within the construction and engineering sectors. Kim et al. (2024) and Amin et al. (2024) establish that structured, credential-verified deployment of personnel directly influences site outcomes, while Putri et al. (2024) and Dabirian et al. (2022) reinforce those findings by demonstrating that labor allocation, when properly managed, significantly affects both cost efficiency and timely project completion. Collectively, these studies affirm that workforce planning is not a peripheral concern but a foundational requirement for operational success. 
At the same time, several studies illustrate a shift from static, manual staffing practices toward dynamic, data-driven approaches. Iskandar et al. (2021) and Biruk (2022) show that optimization models and simulation techniques can substantially improve decision-making in personnel assignment, while Wang et al. (2021) extends this discussion by addressing workforce allocation under crisis conditions, such as the COVID-19 pandemic. These contributions suggest that modern project management increasingly depends on systems capable of adapting to fluctuating demands and unforeseen disruptions, rather than relying solely on conventional, experience-based staffing decisions. 
Local studies further contextualize these findings within the Philippine setting. Carillo et al. (2025) and Cantara et al. (2022) reveal that government and engineering offices continue to experience staffing insufficiencies and inefficient resource distribution, often due to the absence of structured workforce planning tools. Hernandez (2025) adds that while digital monitoring platforms such as the City Engineering Project Monitoring System have improved certain aspects of project oversight, they remain limited in scope, particularly in integrating personnel credentialing with real-time allocation and reporting functions. This indicates that although digitalization efforts are underway locally, they have yet to fully address the interconnected challenges of workforce management and project monitoring. 
Taken together, the literature reveals a consistent gap: no existing system, whether foreign or local, offers a unified platform that integrates credentialed personnel records, skill-based multi-stage allocation, field-to-office reporting, and centralized document management within a single governed structure. Existing solutions tend to address these components in isolation, either focusing on optimization algorithms, workforce planning models, or monitoring systems, but rarely as a cohesive whole. It is this gap that the developed system directly addresses, drawing on the strengths identified across the reviewed studies, structured deployment, dynamic allocation logic, and digital monitoring, while consolidating them into a single, integrated platform designed to improve efficiency, accountability, and responsiveness in construction project workforce management. 
METHODOLOGY
Technical Background 

Technologies to be Used  
The system was developed using technologies selected to support its functionalities, user roles, data management, and web-based accessibility. The frontend was developed using React, a JavaScript library that supports the creation of reusable interface components. React was used to develop the different modules of the system, including the personnel database, project monitoring, workforce allocation, document repository, and notification dashboard. The system also implemented role-based navigation to provide Administrators, Office Project Managers, Site Project Managers, and Clients with interfaces appropriate to their assigned responsibilities.
The backend was developed using Node.js and the Express.js framework to manage application programming interfaces (APIs), process user requests, and facilitate communication between the frontend and database. These technologies supported the implementation of system functionalities, including project management, workforce assignments, progress reporting, and document handling. User authentication was implemented using JSON Web Tokens (JWT) to manage authenticated access, while role-based authorization was used to restrict system operations according to the permissions assigned to each user.
The system utilized Supabase PostgreSQL as its relational database management system to store and organize records related to engineering personnel, subcontractor companies, construction projects, workforce assignments, project progress, documents, and audit logs. The database structure-maintained relationships among records to support data consistency and minimize unnecessary duplication. The system also included database backup and recovery functionality to support the preservation and restoration of operational data, subject to the capabilities of the implemented backup mechanism.
For deployment, the system utilized Vercel, a cloud-based hosting platform that made the web application accessible through the internet. The React frontend and Node.js backend were deployed within the project's Vercel environment, while Supabase provided the hosted PostgreSQL database. A custom domain registered through GoDaddy was configured with dedicated subdomains to provide separate access points for different user roles. This setup allowed authorized users to access the system through web browsers without requiring installation on their devices.
The system was developed with a responsive web design to support accessibility across desktop computers, laptops, tablets, and smartphones. It was designed to operate through modern web browsers, including Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox, and Safari, on supported operating systems such as Windows, Android, and iOS. An internet connection was required to access the system, as offline functionality and a standalone mobile application were not included in its scope.

Requirements Analysis
As established in the Project Context, the existing manual processes of the company presented several operational challenges related to workforce allocation, project monitoring, communication, and document management. The requirements analysis identified these challenges and translated them into functional requirements that guided the development of the proposed system.
First, the existing workforce allocation process lacked a centralized and automated mechanism for assigning engineering personnel to construction projects. The manual approach made it difficult to consistently evaluate personnel based on their experience level, availability, and area of specialization, potentially resulting in inefficient workforce distribution. Second, project progress monitoring relied heavily on manual reporting and consolidation by Project Managers, which limited the Administrator's ability to access timely and organized information regarding ongoing construction projects. Third, project updates followed a reporting hierarchy involving the Site Project Manager, Office Project Manager, Administrator, and Client. The manual exchange of information between these roles could result in reporting delays, inconsistencies, and difficulties in tracking submitted updates. Lastly, project-related documents, subcontractor information, and personnel assignment records were maintained through separate physical and digital files, making information retrieval, organization, and record management more challenging.
These identified challenges served as the basis for establishing the system's functional requirements, particularly in automated workforce allocation, centralized project monitoring, structured progress reporting, and project document management.

Requirements Documentation
The proposed system was developed and deployed as a web-based platform accessible to all authorized users from any location with internet connectivity. Access was governed by a four-role structure covering the Administrator, Office Project Manager, Site Project Manager, and Client, each directed to a role-specific dashboard with permissions aligned to their operational responsibilities within the firm. Core functions included engineering personnel management, subcontractor company management, project monitoring, automated workforce allocation, field-to-office progress reporting, document centralization, and on-demand report generation, all consolidated within a single governed platform.
Workforce allocation was handled through an automated multi-stage module embedded in the project creation and deployment workflow. The system presented the Administrator with a recommended Office Project Manager upon project creation, presented the confirmed OPM with a recommended Site Project Manager lineup for review, and presented the confirmed SPM with recommended subcontractor deployments for review, with human confirmation required at every stage before the process advanced. Personnel recommendations were generated based on specialization, license standing, experience level, and current availability.
Field progress reporting followed a structured review workflow in which Site Project Manager submissions had to be reviewed and approved by the Office Project Manager before any update was reflected in the official project timeline or made visible to the Client. All system activity was logged and retained within a centralized database, ensuring an auditable and consolidated record of operations across all user roles.

Use Case Model of the 10BuildLine System
The use case model illustrated how four actors interacted with the Web-Based Project Monitoring and Workforce Allocation System: the Administrator, the Office Project Manager, the Site Project Manager, and the Client. Each actor’s permissions depended on their role and, where applicable, the projects assigned or linked to their account.
The Administrator managed user accounts, engineering credential records, and the subcontractor company directory. During project intake, the Administrator entered project details, linked a client account, reviewed the system’s recommendations for the Office Project Manager and phase team, and submitted the intake to the assigned Office Project Manager. The Administrator approved or rejected requests from the Lead Site Project Manager to add or replace subcontractor companies when approval was required. The Administrator could also review checklist evidence submitted by Site Project Managers and either approve it or return it for correction. Other functions included monitoring projects and system activity, viewing reports, managing archived records, and requesting and monitoring backups.
The Office Project Manager (OPM) reviewed assigned project intakes and either accepted them or returned them to the Administrator with requested changes. The OPM reviewed the recommended Site Project Manager phase team and could adjust it within the system’s qualification, availability, and phase coverage rules. For assigned projects, the OPM uploaded required documents, monitored progress, requested updates from Site Project Managers, and approved or returned submitted checklist evidence. The OPM also reviewed rework requests, reviewed and released site photos for Client viewing, and accessed monthly project reports.
The Site Project Manager (SPM) accepted or declined assigned phase responsibilities. Within their assigned projects and phases, the SPM submitted checklist evidence, field progress updates, site issues, and notes. The SPM could also upload site photos and view project documents and progress. The Lead Site Project Manager coordinated subcontractor company and foreman assignments by phase, recorded company responses, and requested Administrator approval for allocation changes that required it. Where the Client provided a subcontractor, the Lead Site Project Manager reviewed and acknowledged the company details and addressed any open issues before requesting project activation.
The Client could view project summaries, verified progress, shared project history, authorized documents, released site photos, and available monthly reports for projects linked to their account. Project record editing, construction document uploads, personnel assignment management, and review of pending field evidence were reserved for authorized staff.
All four actors could sign in, sign out, view notifications, and update notification preferences. For account recovery, the Office Project Manager, Site Project Manager, and Client could submit a password-reset request for the Administrator to process. The Administrator used a separate recovery-code process for their own account.

Figure 24: Level 1 Data Flow Diagram of the 10BuildLine System
The Level 1 data flow diagram shows how users, internal processes, data stores, and the external backup worker exchange information. The flows cover account access, project intake, assignments, evidence review, client reporting, and backups.
The Level 1 data flow diagram illustrated how the Web-Based Project Monitoring and Workforce Allocation System for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation exchanged data with the Administrator, Office Project Manager (OPM), Site Project Manager (SPM), Client, and external backup worker. The diagram represented seven processes and six logical data stores.
Manage Accounts, Sign-In, and Sessions (1.0). Users submitted sign-in credentials, and the system checked their account details and roles before granting access to the appropriate workspace. The Administrator managed user accounts, while users could update their notification preferences. Account, role, session, and preference data were maintained in D1.
Manage Project Intake and Team Planning (2.0). The Administrator entered project details, linked a Client account, and reviewed the recommended OPM and phase team. The system used personnel qualifications and availability to generate recommendations. The Administrator confirmed the initial selection and submitted the intake to the assigned OPM for review.
Confirm Assignments and Activate the Project (3.0). The assigned OPM reviewed the intake, and the assigned SPMs responded to their phase assignments. The Lead Site Project Manager coordinated subcontractor and foreman assignments. Before activation, the system checked the required documents, assignment confirmations, and applicable subcontractor requirements. It saved the project checklist snapshot before marking the project active and setting the first phase as current.
Manage Site Work and Evidence (4.0). SPMs submitted checklist evidence, progress updates, site issues, and photographs for their assigned projects and phases. The system stored the submitted files and related records and made evidence available to authorized reviewers.
Review Progress, Handoff, and Archive Projects (5.0). The assigned OPM or Administrator reviewed checklist evidence and either approved it or returned it for correction. Approved evidence contributed to verified progress. The system checked the requirements for opening the next phase and for archiving a project after completion.
Provide Client Views and Reports (6.0). Clients requested information for projects linked to their accounts. The system returned the project overview, verified progress, shared history, authorized documents, released site photographs, and available monthly reports within their access permissions.
Monitor Backups and Restore Tests (7.0). The Administrator could request a backup and view backup status and recorded restore-test results. The external backup worker processed backup requests and reported the outcomes to the system, which stored the corresponding records in D6.
The six logical data stores represented accounts, roles, sessions, and preferences (D1), projects, phases, assignments, and project status (D2), personnel, licenses, subcontractors, and workforce information (D3), project documents, checklist evidence, and photographs (D4), progress, reporting data, history, audit records, and notifications (D5), and backup runs and restore-test records (D6). These stores grouped related data for the diagram. Significant activities were recorded with timestamps to support auditing.
Development
The researchers developed 10BuildLine in Agile iterations. They built the web interface with React and TypeScript and implemented the application programming interface with Node.js and Express. They used Supabase PostgreSQL for project, account, and assignment records and Supabase Storage for uploaded documents, site photographs, and checklist evidence.
The team implemented the workflow from project intake through workforce assignment, field evidence reviews, and client reporting. The server checked each user’s role and project access before processing requests. When the assigned Office Project Manager approved evidence for a required checklist activity, the system recalculated verified progress. The Client workspace showed verified progress and records shared with that client’s linked project. The screenshots below show the resulting workspaces.

Description of Prototype
10buildline is a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system developed for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. The preliminary design organized the system into four role-based workspaces. Administrators register projects, manage accounts, review proposed personnel assignments, and monitor active sites. Office Project Managers confirm assignments, track schedules and phase progress, and review evidence submitted by the site team. Site Project Managers view their assigned phases, report site issues, and submit photographs and other evidence for checklist activities. The Lead Site Project Manager coordinates subcontractor assignments based on availability. Clients use a separate portal to view published progress updates, site photographs, and shared documents.
The prototype requires an internet-connected computer or mobile device with a web browser. Site Project Managers also need access to a camera or existing image files to submit photographic evidence. The front end was developed using React, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS, with Vite as the build tool. The back end uses Node.js and Express. Project and account records are stored in PostgreSQL, while uploaded photographs and documents are stored in Supabase Storage. The application is configured for deployment on Vercel.
Automated functional tests checked key workflows in 10buildline, including role-based sign-in, project intake, personnel and subcontractor allocation, construction phase assignments, and in-app notifications. The tests also checked whether submitted site evidence could be approved or returned for correction, whether approved activities contributed to recorded progress, and whether clients could access the updates and documents released to them.
During the proposed pilot, users will perform tasks in their respective workspaces and provide feedback on the system’s functionality, ease of use, and suitability for the company’s project monitoring and workforce allocation processes.
Description of Prototype
10buildline is a web-based project monitoring and workforce allocation system developed for 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. Its preliminary design organized the system into four role-based workspaces. Administrators register projects, manage accounts, review proposed personnel assignments, and monitor active sites. Office Project Managers confirm assignments, track schedules and phase progress, and review evidence submitted by the site team. Site Project Managers view their assigned phases, report site issues, and submit photographs and other evidence for checklist activities. The Lead Site Project Manager coordinates subcontractor assignments based on the trades required for each phase, verified capabilities, and availability. Clients use a separate portal to view published progress updates, site photographs, and shared documents.
The prototype requires an internet-connected computer or mobile device with a web browser. Site Project Managers also need access to a camera or existing image files to submit photographic evidence. The front end was developed using React, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS, with Vite as the build tool. The back end uses Node.js and Express. Project and account records are stored in PostgreSQL, while uploaded photographs and documents are stored in Supabase Storage. The application is configured for deployment on Vercel.
Automated functional testing covered sign-in, project intake, workforce allocation, phase assignments, progress tracking, evidence submission and review, notifications, and client access. These tests checked whether each role could perform its assigned actions and whether records followed the expected workflow. During the proposed pilot, users will complete tasks in their respective workspaces and provide feedback on the system’s functionality, usability, and fit with the company’s workflow.

Implementation Plan
The implementation of 10buildline will begin with the deployment of the web application and its back-end services. The proponents will configure the Vercel hosting environment, connect the application to PostgreSQL and Supabase Storage, and verify that users can sign in, open their assigned workspaces, and upload and retrieve files. Users will access the system through a web browser on their office computers or mobile devices. The Administrator will create role-based accounts and prepare the pilot project’s details, schedule, personnel records, and subcontractor records.
The proposed rollout will begin with one construction project. The proponents will orient the Administrator, Office Project Manager, Site Project Managers, and participating client on their respective tasks. The office team will use the system to confirm assignments and review progress submissions, while the site team will submit checklist evidence and report issues from the field. The client will use the portal to view published updates and shared documents. The proponents will review feedback from the pilot, address issues, and verify the affected workflows before extending the rollout to additional projects.
Implementation will require office computers, camera-equipped mobile devices for field staff, internet access, and access to the hosting, database, and file storage services. The company’s office and selected construction site will serve as the locations for orientation and pilot use. Supporting materials will include user guides, the pilot project schedule, phase checklists, and sample documents for training. Before the pilot begins, the proponents and project team will confirm that field staff have sufficient connectivity to upload site evidence, that accounts are assigned to the correct project and phases, and that client access is limited to information approved for sharing.

Conclusion
The evaluation of 10buildline showed that the system supported the project monitoring and workforce allocation tasks assessed at 10 Builders Development and Construction Corporation. The survey included 81 respondents across four user roles: one Administrator, 23 Office Project Managers, 50 Site Project Managers, and seven Clients. Every survey response across the five objectives and the overall feedback section was either Agree or Strongly Agree. All 81 respondents strongly agreed that the system was easy to use and that they were satisfied with their overall experience.
The results for centralized records and workforce allocation reflected the work assigned to each role. The Administrator strongly agreed that engineers and subcontractor companies were easy to find by name and that the system displayed engineers recorded work experience. Office Project Managers strongly agreed that they could easily find assigned project records and see which Site Project Managers were assigned to each phase and who had accepted their assignments. Site Project Managers strongly agreed that the system showed their assigned phases and responsibilities. For the item on whether the system clearly indicated that an assignment needed a response, 49 of 50 Site Project Managers (98%) selected Strongly Agree and one (2%) selected Agree.
The results for project progress reporting and review reflected how updates move between the field, the office, and the Client portal. Site Project Managers strongly agreed that they could identify activities requiring supporting files, attach files, see when submitted work was awaiting review, and view the reason when work was returned. Office Project Managers strongly agreed that submitted updates were easy to find, supporting files were easy to open, and progress updates were easy to approve. Clients strongly agreed that the current construction phase was clearly shown and that shared updates were easy to find in the project history. Ratings were more varied for the clarity of overall project progress. Four of seven Clients (57.14%) selected Agree and three (42.86%) selected Strongly Agree.
The findings for role-based access and system support features showed that respondents could use the functions available in their respective workspaces. The activity log received a wider range of ratings than the other support features. Three Office Project Managers (13.04%) and 11 Site Project Managers (22%) selected Agree that previous project activities were easy to find, while the rest in each group selected Strongly Agree. The Administrator also selected Agree on finding project documents and reviewing previous progress updates. These results identify where the system could make information easier to retrieve and present overall progress more clearly to Clients.
Taken together, the survey results support the suitability of 10buildline for the workflows evaluated in the study. The system gave the participating users one platform to coordinate phase assignments, submit and review site work, and share project information with Clients. The results also identify specific areas for refinement as the company uses the system across its projects. 
Recommendations
1. Construction Cost and Budget Management. Future researchers may develop a financial management module that records project budgets, actual expenses, and variation costs. This would allow project teams to examine financial performance alongside construction progress.
2. Materials and Inventory Management. Future researchers may introduce a system for tracking material requests, deliveries, stock levels, and consumption. This could help identify material shortages, excess purchases, and discrepancies between delivered and used quantities.
3. Construction Safety Management. Future researchers may develop a dedicated module for reporting hazards, recording incidents and near misses, and tracking corrective actions. This would support safety oversight as a separate function from progress and quality monitoring.
4. Building Information Modeling Integration. Future researchers may investigate linking project records to an interactive three-dimensional building model. Users could associate documents, photographs, and issues with specific building elements to make project information easier to locate and understand.
5. Post-Construction Maintenance and Warranty Management. Future researchers may extend the system to support building operations after project turnover. This could include warranty records, defect reports, maintenance schedules, and service requests from building owners.
6. Environmental and Construction Waste Monitoring. Future researchers may develop tools for recording construction waste, disposal methods, water consumption, and energy use. These records could support studies on resource efficiency and sustainable construction practices.
7. Document Scanning and Text Recognition. Future researchers may integrate camera-based document scanning so users can capture physical construction documents directly within the system. The feature could detect page edges, correct perspective, improve readability, and combine multiple pages into a PDF. Optical character recognition (OCR) could make scanned text searchable and suggest document details for users to review before saving. This would support digitizing paper records beyond uploading existing files. 
8. Subcontractor Laborer Management. Future researchers may develop a module for maintaining subcontractor laborer records, including trade classifications, site assignments, attendance, and daily deployment. This would help project teams verify the workforce supplied by each subcontractor and compare actual manpower with project requirements.
9. Location-Based Project Monitoring. Future researchers may integrate an interactive map displaying project locations and their corresponding status, progress, and assigned teams. Geotagged site photographs and updates could provide additional context for field reports, helping authorized personnel oversee geographically dispersed construction sites and coordinate site visits.
